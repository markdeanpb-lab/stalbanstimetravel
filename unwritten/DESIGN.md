# The Unwritten: Chapter One. Design

> **Spoilers.** This document contains every solution in the chapter. It is not loaded by the game.

## 1. Setting

**Brindle, Hertfordshire.** A fictional market town six miles up the line from St Albans, with a railway embankment, a mill on the River Brin, a telephone exchange, and a transport café at the Five Ways junction. It is the late 1950s and early 1960s. Cars, lorries, telephone kiosks, wireless sets and convector heaters are all ordinary things.

**The cemetery.** Brindle Cemetery, Holloway Lane. It has a small chapel with a glass-fronted noticeboard, a groundskeeper's hut with a wireless, railings around the old Victorian section, a yew, a bench and a war memorial cross.

**The present.** **Saturday 2 November 1963, All Souls' Day, about 4.20 pm.** The light is going, there's a fine drizzle, and families are visiting graves. The date never changes. Only what lies in the cemetery on that afternoon changes.

**The four deaths** fall between December 1955 and January 1963, a span of seven years. Mourners overlap: the same families, the same café, the same exchange.

|---|---|---|---|---|

## 2. Cast

### Harold "Hal" Brisk (3 March 1897 to 9 December 1955)

- **Epitaph:** *He mended what others threw away.*
- **Who he was:** He repaired wireless sets and electrical goods. He is deaf in his left ear, so he keeps his own wireless turned up loud. He was meticulous and stubborn. He took on a Saturday girl, Iris Wren, at fourteen and taught her to solder.
- **His goal in his final minutes:** Three Pell "Comet" convector heaters came back to him scorched that autumn. Pell Electric, a Brindle firm, brushed off his letters. Tonight he has a fourth Comet on test on his rear bench. He wants to catch its thermostat sticking, in writing, in his test notebook. Then he will go home to his sister for supper.
- **What actually happens:** The thermostat sticks at about 9.40 pm. His loud wireless masks the buzz. The Comet sets the cotton waste on the bench alight while he is writing tickets at the front counter. He fights the fire and is driven out into the yard. Then he goes back in for his notebook. First he tries the back door, which is nearest the bench, but it is bolted, so he goes in through the smoke-filled front. He dies at the rear bench.
- **Mourners in the original 1963:**
  - **Vera Brisk**, his sister, 61. She believes his paraffin stove started the fire ("I told him a hundred times") and that he went back in for the cash box ("Thirty years of 'mind the takings'"). **She is wrong on both counts.** The fire officer's report says the stove was upright and its reservoir intact, the seat of the fire was the rear bench, and the cash box was found unopened on the front counter.
  - **Iris**, before her own death, left a card that still sits in the urn: *"For Mr B., who taught me which end of the iron is hot. I."*

### Dr Margaret Ashdown (14 July 1911 to 15 February 1958)

- **Epitaph:** *She went where she was called.*
- **Who she was:** A general practitioner, and since 1957 Brindle's part-time Medical Officer of Health. She was brisk, funny and overworked.
- **Her goal in her final minutes:** At 11.40 pm the exchange put through a call from the Five Ways kiosk. Six-year-old Davy Wren has croup and is struggling to breathe. His sister will wait at the kiosk to guide the doctor down Tanner's Row in the fog. Dr Ashdown left the Rotary dinner at once and is driving fast.
- **What actually happens:** The junction lamp has been out for six weeks. Len Mott's lorry pulls out of the café yard across the junction. At the same moment Iris, aged 18, sees headlights and runs into the road to flag the car down. Dr Ashdown swerves to miss her and slides on the ice into the side of the lorry.
- **Mourners in the original 1963:**
  - **Frank Ashdown**, her husband. He believes she had two sherries at the Rotary dinner and drove carelessly. ("I should have driven her.") **He is wrong.** She left before the toast, sober, on an emergency call. Her call book page, which he keeps in his wallet, says so, but he has never read it closely.
  - **Len Mott**, the lorry driver. He believes she came out of the fog doing fifty. That is true, but it is not the cause. He also half-remembers that "she swerved first, like something was in the road", and that part is right.

### Kathleen "Kath" Wren (2 August 1915 to 22 September 1961)

- **Epitaph:** *Home by six.*
- **Who she was:** A widow (Sgt Thomas Wren was killed in 1944) and laundry supervisor at the Cottage Hospital. She is the mother of Iris and Davy. She is practical and never late.
- **Her goal in her final minutes:** To get home by six with Davy's tenth-birthday present under her arm. It is a crystal-set kit she has saved for since Easter, and Iris has promised to build it with him.
- **What actually happens:** A cloudburst has flooded the Brin Lane underpass because the culvert grate is choked with leaves. A stranger, Cyril Dodd, is trapped in his stalled car because the door frame has twisted. Kath wades in and wrenches the door open. In the struggle her parcel falls into the water. She gets Cyril to the steps, then goes back for the parcel. The rising water pins her against the culvert grate.
- **Mourners in the original 1963:**
  - **Win Halloran**, her sister. She believes Kath tried to wade through to get home faster ("always in a hurry"). **She is wrong.** Kath went in for a stranger.
  - **Davy Wren**, 12. "She went back for something. Nobody'll tell me what." He is right, but he doesn't know what it was.
  - **Cyril Dodd**, the man she saved. He leaves a card: *"To the lady who came into the water for me. I never knew your name until the inquest. C. D."* The card contradicts Win.

## 3. The ghost

**Iris Wren** (11 May 1939 to 19 January 1963) was a night telephonist at Brindle exchange.

- **How she died (original):** It was the Big Freeze of 1963, and every heater in Brindle was running night and day. The exchange's rest room had a Pell Comet. At about 3 am on 19 January its thermostat stuck and the rest room caught fire. Iris was alone on the night board, with burst-pipe calls and a maternity case coming in. She kept connecting calls until the smoke took her. Her epitaph reads *"Number, please."*
- **Her connection to each victim:**
  - **Hal Brisk:** She was his Saturday girl from 1953 to 1955. She learned to solder at his bench and later taught Davy. **Scene A clue:** a job ticket and the apron on the hook.
  - **Dr Ashdown:** Iris made the 11.40 pm call for Davy and waited at the Five Ways kiosk. She is the "something in the road" that made the doctor swerve, and has carried that guilt ever since. **Scene B clue:** her younger self is in the scene, with a note in her mitten initialled "I.W."
  - **Kath Wren:** Kath was her mother, and the parcel is from "Mum and Iris". **Scene C clue:** the parcel tag. If the kiosk rings, Kath answers it with "Iris? Is that you, love?"
- **Why she can't be entered:** Her death is the latest of the four and is the sum of the others. It can only be changed through consequences.
- **How it changes:**
  - A **Comet recall** in March 1958 prevents every Comet fire, including hers. The recall needs Hal's evidence **and** Dr Ashdown alive to put it before the Health Committee on 3 March 1958.
  - **If Hal survives but there is no recall,** he fits his own thermal cut-outs to every Comet he can reach, including the exchange's, through his old friend Mr Fenn, the night supervisor. He never knew the Wrens bought a second-hand Comet in December 1962. The exchange never burns. Instead Tanner's Row burns on **2 February 1963**. Iris gets Davy (and Kath, if she's alive) out and dies on the stairs. This is a **different death**.

**Clues planted in the cemetery from the first minute:**
- A wren carved on the illegible stone.
- The Wren family plaque beside it ("Sgt Thomas Wren 1912 to 1944, remembered by Kath, Iris and David").
- Davy calling the stone "Sis".
- Iris's card at Hal's grave.
- The rain-smudged clipping at the foot of her stone ("Miss I——s W——n, 23, telephonist").

## 6. Scenes (three phases: inspect, countdown, resolution)

All scene logic is a deterministic fixed-step simulation (20 ticks per second) shared by live play and the resolver. Each possessable object can be used once per run, and each person can be whispered to once per run. A whisper makes the person walk to the point of interest nearest the ghost (or the ghost's own tile), look for a few seconds, react, then carry on with their routine.

### Scene A: Brisk's Wireless, Fri 9 Dec 1955, from 9.38 pm (countdown 3 minutes)

**Objects:**
- **Wireless** (possess: switch it off)
- **Telephone** (possess: ring)
- **Fuse board** (possess: throw the bench circuit)
- **Back-door bolt** (possess: draw the bolt)
- **Paraffin stove** (possess: turn down the wick; this is the misread cause)

**Inspectables:**
- The Comet: its thermostat should click off, but instead it buzzes.
- The notebook.
- The cash box.
- The job tickets: the café's Murphy set, and *"Tell Iris it's ready"*.
- The Saturday girl's apron, initialled I.W.

**Thin barrier:** the plank partition between the shop and the workshop.

**Hal's routine:**

| Time | Where | What he does |
|---|---|---|
| 0–24 s | Bench | Writes |
| 26–38 s | Shelf | Sorts sets |
| 42–66 s | Counter | Counts the takings |
| 68–80 s | Telephone | Rings Vera ("another half hour, Vee") |
| 84–96 s | Shelf | Works beside the loud wireless |
| 100 s on | Counter | Writes tickets |

**The Comet:**
- At 60 s the thermostat sticks and a buzz starts, masked by the wireless.
- At 100 s it smokes.
- At 108 s it ignites.

**The fire:**
1. Hal smells smoke and fights it at the bench, breathing smoke.
2. He flees by the nearest open door.
3. "My book!" He tries the back door first, then the front, and goes back in.

**Smoke exposure decides the outcome:** lethal on site, severe (dies 12 Dec), or light (survives).

**Solutions:**
- **Silence the wireless** before about 96 s. He hears the buzz while he's at the shelf, catches the stuck thermostat and unplugs it: **survived_proof**.
- **Ring the telephone.** He thinks it's Vera nagging and packs up. If the Comet has already stuck when he unplugs it, he notices (**survived_proof**). If not, he unplugs it without looking (**survived_noproof**).
- **Throw the bench circuit.** He resets it and then checks the Comet. If it has stuck, **survived_proof**. If not, it just pauses the heater.
- **Whisper at the Comet** after it sticks: **survived_proof**. **Whisper at the notebook:** he pockets it, so when the fire comes he doesn't go back: **survived_proof**, and the shop burns.
- **Draw the back-door bolt** before the fire drives him out. He escapes through it, dashes back in for the book (the drawer is a step from the door) and comes straight out: **survived_proof**.
  - If you draw it only after he has fled through the smoky front shop, he comes round and goes in by the back, already badly smoked: **died_later**. Vera gets the notebook.
  - Once he has gone back in through the front, it is too late.
- **Misread cause:** turning down the paraffin stove (Vera's theory) does nothing, because it was never the cause. Whispering him to the cash box (Vera's other theory) makes him pocket the takings, and he still goes back for the notebook and dies. Before entry, the fire report clipping (stove upright, seat of fire at the rear bench, cash box unopened at the front) and Hal's letter about the Comet test point to the truth.

### Scene B: Five Ways, Sat 15 Feb 1958, from 11.46 pm (countdown about 2 min 50 s)

**Objects:**
- **Kiosk telephone** (possess: ring)
- **Café door latch** (possess: release; the door bangs in the wind)
- **Café wireless** (possess: switch on; *only if Hal is alive*. Otherwise it is a dead set with a card reading "Lost in the Brisk fire")

**Inspectables:**
- The broken junction lamp.
- Len's lorry and delivery sheet ("Tilbury by 3 am").
- Iris's note ("Dr A coming. WAIT AT THE BOX. I.W.").
- The kiosk's pencilled numbers.

**Thin barrier:** the café's side wall.

**Routines:**
- **Len:** tea until about 98 s, pays, walks to the cab, warms the engine, then pulls across the junction from about 136 s to 156 s.
- **Iris:** paces between the kiosk and the kerb. When she sees headlights (about 128 s) she runs into the road if the car is still at least 4 s away.
- **The car:** reaches the junction at 150 s.
- **Bert:** stays behind the counter.

**Collision rule at 150 s:**

| Iris in the road? | Lorry in the junction? | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Yes | Yes | Swerves into the lorry (original) |
| Yes | No | Swerves into the pole (**different death**) |
| No | Yes | Slides into the lorry |
| No | No | Pulls up safely |

**Solutions:**
- **Iris:** ring the kiosk between about 126 s and 134 s, so she is inside answering silence until it's too late to run out. Or whisper her to a point away from the road at the right moment.
- **Len:** he needs at least 13 s of delay. A whisper toward the back of the lorry plus the banging café door is enough. **The café wireless alone is enough** ("Hang about, is that the Cooper fight?"), but it exists only if Hal lived. This is the **changed solution**.
- **Misread cause:** stopping only the lorry (the cause everyone blames) still kills her, because she swerves into the pole. Len's account at the grave ("she swerved first, like something was in the road") and the clipping ("skid marks show the car swerved left before impact") point to the girl in the road.

### Scene C: Brin Lane underpass, Fri 22 Sep 1961, from 5.58 pm (countdown about 2 min)

**Objects:**
- **Car door latch** (possess: release)
- **Culvert grate latch** (possess: release; behind railings, a thin barrier the ghost must pass)
- **Kiosk at the west steps** (possess: ring)

**Inspectables:**
- The "liable to flooding" sign.
- The choked grate.
- Cyril's car.
- Kath's parcel tag ("For Davy, 10 tomorrow, love Mum & Iris x").
- The rising water.

**Routines:**
- **Cyril:** trapped, sounding the horn every 12 s.
- **Kath:** pushes her bicycle along Brin Lane, reaches the mouth at about 42 s, wades in, and works the jammed door for 25 s. She gets Cyril to the west steps, catches her breath for about 5 s, then goes back for the parcel, which is drifting to the grate.
- **The water:** rises about 0.034 per second. At level 4 or above at the grate, she is pinned. Pinned for 8 s means she drowns.

**Solutions:**
- **Release the car door before Kath wades in.** Cyril wades out and meets her at the mouth, and they go round by the footbridge: **survived**, with no exposure. This is the plain kindness.
- **Release it while she's working the door.** He's out sooner and the water is still low when she goes back: **survived**.
- **Release the grate early:** the water drains and she is **survived**. **Release it while she is pinned:** she is pulled free, chilled to the bone, and dies of **pneumonia** two weeks later. **Release it after she gets Cyril to the steps but before she goes back:** the parcel is sucked away, she doesn't go back, and she is **survived**.
- **Ring the kiosk** while she's at the steps. She answers ("Iris? Is that you, love?"). By the time she hangs up, the parcel is gone: **survived**. **Whisper** to her at Cyril on the steps, which has the same effect.

## 7. The rewritten cemetery

Consequences are shown through things the player can see, never through a "timeline changed" notice. A few examples:

- **Hal survives:** his plot is turf, and a robin sits where the urn was. Hal (66) and Vera stand at Iris's stone. Without the recall: *"I fitted a cut-out to every Comet in this town I could lay hands on. I never thought to ask about hers."* With proof but Ashdown dead: *"I took my book to Dr Ashdown. Then Five Ways. Nobody else would read past page one."*
- **Dr Ashdown survives:** her plot is gone, and so is Kath's. Dr Ashdown is at the war memorial selling poppies. If there's no proof: *"Three Comet fires in Brindle that winter. Hal Brisk swore the thermostats stuck, God rest him. Nobody had it on paper."* Kath and Davy stand at Iris's stone, and the person who used to grieve at Kath's grave now visits Iris with Kath beside him.
- **Kath survives:** her grave is gone; Kath and Davy are at Iris's stone together. Win isn't there. Cyril isn't there.
- **Different deaths:** each changes the inscription, the clipping, the flowers, and what the mourners believe. Vera knows about the notebook. Frank knows a girl was in the road. Win knows Kath brought a stranger out.
- **Iris lives:** the illegible stone is gone and a wren sits on the railing. The living Iris, Kath and Davy are at Sgt Wren's plaque; Hal and Vera are by the chapel; Dr Ashdown is at the memorial.

**The keeper's wireless** gives a different bulletin in each world:
- Comet Mark II advertisements in the original.
- A report on the Comet scrappage scheme after the recall.
- A Brin Lane flood warning if the culvert was never cleared.

## 8. Charges, re-entry and saving

- The player starts with **two journeys**. Three graves are enterable.
- Investigating the cemetery is free and unlimited.
- The confirmation dialog explains the cost before entry. The charge is spent when the scene loads.
- Restarting a scene (R) is free and unlimited. **Return to the cemetery** commits that run's action log.
- **Decision: a grave cannot be re-entered.** Each grave can be entered once. This keeps every death's commitment weighty, and it makes the committed interventions a set keyed by scene, so order independence holds by construction. The game says this on every grave dialog and in the opening hints.
- A grave whose occupant is alive disappears and cannot be entered. A scene that no longer happens makes any action recorded there moot.
- The game saves to `localStorage` after each commit, inside try/catch. **New game** asks for confirmation.

## 9. Journal and knowledge

- The ghost remembers every timeline. Journal entries are never deleted. Observations from earlier worlds are tagged "in another timeline".
- **Clues** are split into **Confirmed** (inspected evidence) and **Theories** (mourners' beliefs and the ghost's guesses). Contradicted theories are struck through with the contradicting clue named.
- **Changes** records the before and after of every altered grave the player has looked at again, including graves that are gone.
- **Who am I?** The name is confirmed when the player holds at least one scene identity clue and at least two cemetery identity clues (wren carving, family plaque, Davy's "Sis", Iris's card at Hal's grave). The stone then becomes legible.
- **How did I die?** This is known once the player reads the clipping or inscription for the *current* world's death.

## 10. Endings

Endings are triggered deliberately at the ghost's own headstone, or where it stood. This is possible at any time once a journey has been made, and the prompt after the second commit says so. The ending is decided from the final timeline and the evidence found. There is no morality score.

1. **Saved** (Iris alive). The ghost is what remains of a life that no longer happened. At the place she woke, the living Iris stops, looks straight through her, and says "Funny, I thought someone said my name." The ghost lets go. The journal's pages empty one by one, because none of it happened. It ends: *"Somewhere a switchboard lights. 'Number, please.'"*
2. **Accepted** (Iris dead, name confirmed, and current cause of death known). The stone is legible. Before choosing, the game lists exactly what will stay as it is: who lives, who doesn't, and how Iris died. The player can then lie down beneath the name or keep walking.
3. **Incomplete** (Iris dead, truth not established). The letters never settle. The ghost ends the night without knowing, and the ending says what remains unknown.

Every ending closes with an **account** of the chain the player actually created: the actions taken and the consequences that followed in the final world. It never mentions untried solutions.

## 12. Presentation

- **View:** isometric canvas with a restrained palette:
  - Slate and moss for the cemetery.
  - Sepia and bench-lamp amber for the workshop.
  - Sodium-less fog blue for Five Ways.
  - Wet green-grey for Brin Lane.
- **Ability markers**, one shape and colour each, shown in the legend in the pause menu:
  - **Pass through:** a dashed doorway, teal.
  - **Inspect:** an eye, amber.
  - **Possess:** a diamond with a spiral, violet.
  - **Whisper:** sound arcs, pale blue.
- **Audio:** all procedural Web Audio:
  - Wind and drizzle.
  - Room tone and the dance band through a band-pass "wireless" filter.
  - Comet buzz and fire crackle.
  - Fog hum, a lorry engine and an approaching car.
  - Rain, rushing water and a train.
  - UK double-ring telephones, the whisper swish, the possess chime, and a chapel bell.
- **Accessibility:**
  - Subtitles with speaker names, in three sizes.
  - Reduced motion: no bobbing, flashes, particles or fades.
  - Pause from anywhere.
  - Touch controls.
